Making a complex manufacturing platform easier to understand.

Redesigning the website to give manufacturing buyers a clearer way to understand the product, explore its capabilities, and move towards a trial or conversation.

Website design, design system
Role
UX/UI Designer
Client

Year

Overview

Canvas Envision helps manufacturers turn PDFs, videos, and 3D models into interactive work instructions. Its customers need accurate guidance to move from engineering teams to frontline workers without becoming disconnected or outdated.

The website redesign was intended to support organic growth, self-service product education, and a future e-commerce journey. Visitors needed to learn through demos, walkthroughs, and technical resources before speaking with sales.

The challenge

The existing experience relied on an outdated template, a confusing mega menu, weak calls to action, and content scattered across legacy and current sites. Company, legal, and support information lacked clear destinations.

The redesign needed to simplify a broad product portfolio while communicating assurance, practical innovation, and urgency.

Approach

Working within the Arch Web Design team, I designed the full responsive website and its supporting mockups. I translated the client requirements into a clearer hierarchy spanning the platform, products, solutions, use cases, AI capability, company information, and learning resources.

I presented the work directly to the client. Feedback on the homepage led us through different visual and colour directions before reaching the approved design; I then extended that direction across the remaining pages using the design system I had established.

Repeated pathways connected product education with trial, assessment, and demo actions without forcing every visitor directly into a sales conversation.

Turning technical capability into a visual story.

Simplify the architecture

The oversized navigation was replaced with distinct routes for products, solutions, use cases, resources, and company content. Each destination had a clear purpose, reducing overlap between legacy and current offerings.

Show the product

Product interfaces, 3D visuals, and step-by-step stories replaced dense feature lists. This made the relationship between source material, authoring, and frontline delivery easier to follow.

Build authority without becoming cold

Editorial serif headlines were paired with practical interface imagery. Controlled colour shifts distinguished product areas while shared typography, spacing, and calls to action kept the experience cohesive.

What shipped

The final Figma delivery covered the homepage, platform and product pages, solutions, use cases, AI storytelling, resources, and company content. Each of the 20 page designs included desktop and mobile layouts, supported by a library of 32 reusable components.

Outcome

The project delivered a fully redesigned web presence with clearer navigation, more consistent conversion paths, and a reusable design foundation. It was designed to help manufacturing buyers learn independently and give the Canvas team a stronger platform for future content, commerce, and optimisation.
