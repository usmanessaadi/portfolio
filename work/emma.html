Making an all-in-one fundraising platform easier to understand.

Redesigning Emma’s website to clearly communicate its event-management platform and guide charities towards a demo.

Website design
Role
UX/UI Designer

Overview

Emma is an event-management and fundraising platform for charities. It brings ticketing, donations, silent auctions, guest management, and reporting together in one system, supported by an account-management team.

The website redesign needed to make that value immediately understandable. Emma wanted prospective customers to discover the platform organically, understand its capabilities without needing a sales explanation, and confidently move towards booking a demo.

The challenge

The existing website did not clearly communicate who Emma was, what the platform included, or why charities should choose it over separate fundraising and event-management tools.

The breadth of the product created an additional design challenge. The experience needed to introduce a large feature set without overwhelming visitors, many of whom were looking for something straightforward and did not consider themselves highly technical.

The brief called for a site that felt exciting, trustworthy, and empowering while keeping demo bookings as the primary conversion goal.

Approach

Working within the Arch Web Design team, I redesigned Emma’s 10-page responsive marketing website using its existing brand identity and product dashboard as foundations. I was responsible for the website’s visual direction, page layouts, responsive designs, and product mockups. The underlying brand and dashboard product design were outside my scope.

I translated the client discovery brief into a clearer hierarchy that introduced Emma as one connected platform before expanding into individual features, pricing, customer stories, and resources. Calls to book a demo appeared at relevant decision points, while secondary pathways supported visitors who needed more information first.

I presented the designs directly to the client and refined the direction through their feedback. The overall structure remained consistent, but the visual system introduced greater colour variety across the site. Product mockups were also adapted for UK and US audiences, including regional language, pricing, and currencies.

Design decisions

Lead with the complete platform

The homepage establishes Emma as an all-in-one solution before introducing individual tools such as silent auctions, ticketing, and donations.

Make complexity approachable

Focused sections, concise copy, and interface mockups help visitors explore the platform without confronting a dense feature list.

Balance energy with credibility

Editorial typography, expressive colour, and product imagery add personality while consistent layouts keep the experience dependable.

What shipped

The final delivery included 10 responsive page designs covering the homepage, features, pricing, company information, customer stories, resources, and conversion pages, with variations for UK and US audiences.

Outcome

The 10-page website was approved by the client and successfully launched. It gave Emma a clearer way to communicate its all-in-one platform while creating stronger routes towards demos and enquiries.
